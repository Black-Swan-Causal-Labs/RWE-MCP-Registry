Search observations, individual reviews, canonical projects, components and public cards have different denominators. Repeated search hits, identical instruction bodies, imported copies and hosted/source aliases must not be counted as separate discoveries by default. Conversely, approval of one component must not approve an entire collection or its held siblings. Automated metadata/homonym screens, exact-copy reconciliation and URL-role screening remain labeled separately from individual source reviews.

Eligibility records describe bounded documented capabilities and limitations. Adjacent scientific tools and necessary research infrastructure are distinguished from direct RWE workflows. Candidate programs were not installed or runtime-tested. Static source inspection can identify contradictions, but documentation-level eligibility is not evidence of analytical validity or clinical suitability. Historical approval can be withdrawn when stronger source evidence contradicts it; the original identity and review history remain retained.

Remaining coverage limitations include the original unamended query matrix and the need for future dated maintenance. The completed search process retains the original matrix limitation, source-access constraints and earlier code-log audit gap. Meeting the revised stopping rule describes this documented search process at a date; it does not guarantee an exhaustive inventory of a changing public ecosystem.
